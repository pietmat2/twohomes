# Who to grill the idea with

Eight people to argue with before building anything else. They are written to
be *structurally* different, not demographically decorative: each one holds a
different position in the system — who keeps the status quo, who pays, who has
the children most, who can refuse — because that position, not their age, is
what makes their objection land.

Use them one at a time. A persona is useful only when it can **kill** the idea;
if a session ends with "good points, let's continue", the persona was written
too kindly.

**What is not in here:** real numbers. Where a figure would change a decision
(how many Danish children live 7/7, how many separations reach
Familieretshuset, the share of fathers with equal residence) it has to come
from Danmarks Statistik or the survey, not from me. Treat every persona as a
hypothesis to test, never as evidence.

## The axes that actually matter

| Axis | Why it changes the product |
| --- | --- |
| Conflict level | Cooperative wants speed; high-conflict wants proof. Opposite products. |
| Who holds the status quo | Consent mechanics favour whoever has the children more. |
| Who pays | The person who would pay and the person the product serves may not be the same. |
| Will the other home join | Decides whether this is a shared tool or a solo tool with a share link. |
| Tech comfort | Decides whether it can be an app at all, or needs to be a link in a text. |
| Stage | In-transition families are the only ones actively shopping. |

## 1. Mette, 34 — Odense. Teacher. Two kids, 6 and 9. 7/7.

Separated 3 years, amicable, shared Google Calendar, settles money on
MobilePay. **The archetype the product is built for — and the one who will
never pay.** Her arrangement works; the app would replace something free that
already works.

> *"What am I buying? We already solved this."*

**Would stay for:** holiday weeks agreed early, the bag list, one place for
school info. **In scope. Test pricing on her before anything else.**

## 2. Line, 38 — Aarhus. Nurse, shifts. Two kids, 7/7 since 2023.

Cooperative but tired. Pays for Netflix and nothing else. Already drowning in
Aula.

> *"We already have one app the school forced on us. You want to be the second?"*

**Would stay for:** fewer apps, not more — ideally something that absorbs the
holiday-week negotiation. **In scope. The volume segment.**

## 3. Jesper, 42 — Copenhagen. Project manager, full time. Kids 9 and 13. Writing only.

High conflict. Eleven unanswered messages. A solicitor who says *document
everything*. The most motivated visitor on the site and the one the product
decided not to serve.

> *"I don't need a nicer way to ask. I need it on the record that I asked and she
> didn't answer for nine days."*

**Would stay for:** timestamps, non-response visibility, tamper-evident
history, export. **Out of scope by decision — but he has the money, and the
page currently neither serves nor honestly turns him away.**

## 4. Sofie, 29 — Vejle. Retail. One child, 4. The child lives with her; the father has every other weekend.

The resident parent. Does ~90% of everything. For her a "shared" tool is
unpaid admin on behalf of someone who contributes little.

> *"Why would I keep a system up to date for a man who sees her four days a month?"*

**Would stay for:** nothing about coordination — only for proof of what he
actually turns up to, which is the high-conflict product again. **The segment
most likely to install, open twice and leave. Test retention here, not sign-ups.**

## 5. Rasmus, 45 — Esbjerg. Electrician. The other home who will not install anything.

He is not a user. He is the reason most co-parenting apps die, and he is a
quarter to a half of every family you reach.

> *"I'm not downloading my ex's app so she can tick a box saying I agreed."*

**Would stay for:** nothing with a login. A read-only link in a text message,
no account, no app, is the entire surface he will ever touch. **Out of scope as
a user, decisive as a constraint — and the reason the share link is a
first-class feature, not a fallback.**

## 6. Emma, 13 — the child. Lives 7/7 between two flats.

In Denmark her view carries weight long before she turns 15, and she is the one
the product claims to be for.

> *"Nobody asked me. Now there's an app where they both log where I sleep."*

**Would stay for:** being able to see her own week, and to say she doesn't want
to go on Wednesday without starting a war. **Not a buyer. The ethical check: if
a feature would embarrass her or be used against her, it fails regardless of
what the parents want.**

## 7. Camilla, 36 — Roskilde. Jesper's new partner. Does the pickups and the packing.

The person who actually operates the schedule in many households, with no legal
standing and no account.

> *"I do the logistics and I'm not allowed to see anything?"*

**Would stay for:** delegated, limited access — her partner's days only,
nothing about the mother. **In scope as a second seat, and a quiet trust
minefield: the mother must never discover the new partner reading her notes.**

## 8. Birgitte, 51 — family mediator, Familieretshuset.

Not a user. A gatekeeper: parents arrive at her desk in the fortnight when they
are actually looking for a tool.

> *"If I recommend your app and one parent later uses its export in court, that's
> on me. Can you tell me, in writing, what it does with a disagreement?"*

**Would stay for:** a clear statement of what the product refuses to do, and no
feature that could be weaponised in a hearing. **Out of scope as a user, the
single best distribution channel, and she will only recommend a product whose
limits are written down.**

## Who is deliberately missing, and why that matters

- **A long-distance or cross-border parent** (one in Denmark, one in Poland —
  two school calendars, two languages, flights). Twohomes already supports two
  countries; nobody has yet argued with it from that position.
- **A parent in the first three months.** The only moment anyone shops for this.
  No persona here is in transition, which means the cast is biased toward
  people whose routine already works — exactly the bias that makes a product
  look less necessary than it is.
- **A father with equal residence and a decent relationship.** The page's voices
  are all women except one sample quote; half the users are men who are not
  Jesper.

## How to run a session

1. Pick **one** persona and give them the artefact as it stands — the live page,
   not a description of it.
2. Make them speak for eight seconds only, then stop. First reactions are the
   product.
3. Ask them the question they would ask, not the question you want asked.
4. Write down the sentence that kills it. If there isn't one, the persona was
   too soft.
5. Decide, in one line, whether the answer is a copy change, a product change,
   or a decision to lose that person on purpose.

Losing someone on purpose is a legitimate outcome and the page should do it in
the first screen, not the fourth.
